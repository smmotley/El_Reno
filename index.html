THE EVIDENCE BEHIND THE MAP

A reconstruction,
with the gaps left visible.

On May 31, 2013, we were chasing near El Reno. This project brings the available records onto one clock to better understand where we were.

01 / Our position

The original 86 camera GPS records are preserved. UTC is a working assumption. Short intervals are linearly interpolated; gaps longer than five minutes stay empty. Interpolation can cut road corners.

02 / The tornado

The NWS survey contains minute-stamped center estimates from 6:04–6:42 PM CDT. The shaded area is the accumulated surveyed path, not the tornado’s footprint at every instant. Neither layer resolves individual subvortices.

03 / The radar

Archived KTLX Level II reflectivity and radial velocity use their actual sweep times. Playback holds the preceding scan; it does not invent radar between scans. Velocity is motion toward or away from the radar, not total wind speed. Base velocity is not dealiased.

04 / Closest approach

Separation is calculated between simultaneous interpolated camera and NWS center positions. The minimum is sampled each second. It is provisional: clock error, sparse GPS, and uncertain tornado centers prevent an exact distance to the tornado’s edge.

In memory of Tim Samaras, Paul Samaras, Carl Young, and all who lost their lives that day.
