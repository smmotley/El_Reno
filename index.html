THE EVIDENCE BEHIND THE MAP

A reconstruction,
with the gaps left visible.

On May 31, 2013, we were chasing near El Reno. This project brings the available records onto one clock to better understand where we were.

01 / Our position

The original 86 camera GPS records are preserved. UTC is a working assumption. Positions are matched to a locally stored OpenStreetMap road network. Between fixes, movement follows connected roads at an inferred average speed. Gaps over five minutes and implausible or unmatched routes stay empty. Current roads are a hypothesis about the route taken in 2013.

02 / The tornado

The NWS survey contains minute-stamped center estimates from 6:04–6:42 PM CDT. The shaded area is the accumulated surveyed path, not the tornado’s footprint at every instant. Neither layer resolves individual subvortices.

03 / The radar

Switch between KTLX WSR-88D and the University of Oklahoma’s AIR research radar. KTLX uses separate low-tilt sweep times. AIR uses its recorded volume coverage midpoint because individual ray times are unavailable. Separate REF and VEL timeline rows mark every available image at its representative acquisition time. Click a tick for its exact time; the slider and step buttons move in 10-second increments. Playback holds the preceding scan until the next image. Velocity is motion toward or away from the radar, not total wind speed. KTLX base velocity is not dealiased. AIR is a reflectivity-only research preview. Its optional approximate NWS alignment fits the user-marked 6:12 feature; archive geolocation remains unverified. Its velocity is excluded because of published 2013 instrument limitations; switch to WSR-88D for velocity.

04 / Closest approach

Separation is calculated between simultaneous inferred road and NWS center positions. The minimum is sampled each second. It is provisional: clock error, sparse GPS, and uncertain tornado centers prevent an exact distance to the tornado’s edge.

In memory of Tim Samaras, Paul Samaras, Carl Young, and all who lost their lives that day.

A SECOND VIEW OF THE STORM

Atmospheric Imaging Radar.

The University of Oklahoma’s AIR collected rapid sector scans of the El Reno tornado. Use the 0.5° map layer or rotate the 3D reflectivity scans, each built from 45 elevation slices. There are 99 recorded volumes, usually 7–8 seconds apart. Geographic alignment remains unverified.

Limited time and view

Reading the AIR recording window…

Blank areas outside the observed sector are missing coverage. With the uncorrected camera clock, AIR starts after the provisional closest approach estimated from your camera GPS.

Why AIR velocity is unavailable

The instrument paper documents technical problems that prevented the authors from presenting radial-velocity estimates before 2014. Although this archive contains a velocity field, we found no documentation resolving that limitation for these 2013 scans. AIR velocity is excluded. Switch to WSR-88D to view its recorded base velocity.

Timing and detail

Each image uses the midpoint of the recorded volume start/end. The original start/end interval is shown beside the scan time. Individual ray timestamps in this archive are unusable. AIR records the elevation slices simultaneously while scanning across azimuth; combining them into 3D does not add a time offset. The 30 m gate spacing does not imply 30 m independent resolution; beam width, distance, and image sampling also matter.

Reading the 3D view

The threshold selects which reflectivity values are visible. Opacity controls their appearance. These echoes are sampled on a coarser display grid; their surfaces are not measured cloud or tornado boundaries. Height is above mean sea level without vertical exaggeration. The optional surface uses public terrain elevations and current roads. Chaser locations follow the same supported GPS/survey interpolation as the map, with approximate ground heights from the terrain model. Symbols are enlarged and drawn in front of radar echoes for readability; this does not mean they are above the storm. Neither the terrain nor these overlays resolve AIR’s uncertain alignment.

Research preview

The “Approx. NWS alignment” switch shifts AIR about 6.64 km east and 0.98 km north in both views. It fits the feature you marked in the first 6:12 PM CDT image to the NWS center estimate. The same shift applies to all scans; times, terrain and chaser positions are unchanged. Turn it off to restore the original archive placement. This is a visual estimate, not a verified radar location or heading correction. The marked circle spans roughly a 350 m radius; this is not a measured error bound. Low-level blockage below 2° is documented for this deployment, so the 0.5° feature is not an independently verified surface center. Do not use this fit to refine vehicle-to-tornado distances. Calibration remains unverified.

CAMERA CLOCK CALIBRATION

Time changes the story.

The camera file uses a UTC “Z” suffix. Its clock has not been independently verified. A correction shifts your track and recalculates the separation estimate; radar and NWS times stay fixed.
