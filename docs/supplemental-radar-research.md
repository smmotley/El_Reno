# Supplemental radar for 31 May 2013 El Reno

Checked 4 November 2026. This document records the availability investigation. An AIR layer was subsequently requested and is being implemented separately; the limited sample inspection described here is not a validation of that implementation. No DOW data request was sent.

**Subsequent review:** AIR velocity is excluded because the primary system paper identifies pre-2014 velocity limitations. Reflectivity is retained only as a research preview with geographic alignment unverified. See [the independent validation](air-validation.md) for the final evidence and product restrictions; the presence of a `VEL` field below is not a finding that it provides reliable winds.

## Best confirmed public download: AIR

The University of Oklahoma's [official curated archive](https://arrc.ou.edu/data.html) explicitly identifies its Atmospheric Imaging Radar (AIR) dataset as observations of the **31 May 2013 El Reno tornado and parent supercell**, from approximately 20–30 km range. This is AIR, not DOW or RaXPol. OU permits research, education, and outreach use with acknowledgment and appropriate radar/publication citations.

**Direct asset:** [20130531_AIR_CFRadial.zip](https://arrc.ou.edu/data/20130531_AIR_CFRadial.zip). HTTP HEAD returned 200, `application/zip`, 7,030,432,500 bytes, and byte-range support. The archive page describes CF/Radial format and links [Kurdzo et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-15-00266.1) for the radar observations.

Remote ZIP-directory inspection found **4,455 NetCDF sweep files, 99 volume timestamps**, from **23:11:56 through 23:24:51 UTC (6:11:56–6:24:51 PM CDT)**. Adjacent volume timestamps differ by 7 seconds (37 cases), 8 seconds (57), or 15 seconds (4). This public subset covers part of the tornado's lifetime; it begins after the current provisional closest-approach estimate from the camera track. Directory timestamps are volume starts, not independently verified ray times.

Limited sample inspection established:

- `cfrad.20130531_231156_AIR_v1_s6.nc` has a **0.5° fixed elevation**. The first sweep is −2°; choosing the first sweep would be incorrect for the requested 0.5° view.
- Sample variables include `DBZ` (reflectivity), `VEL` (radial velocity away from the instrument), and `WIDTH` (spectrum width).
- The first sampled sweep has 239 rays over roughly a 119° sector, approximately 0.5° azimuth sampling, and **30 m range-gate spacing**. Gate spacing is not a claim of independent spatial resolution or antenna beamwidth.
- Sample radar coordinates are approximately 35.31210, −97.74460; metadata altitude is 385 m.
- Global time coverage is readable: the first sampled sweep gives 23:11:56–23:12:04 UTC. **Per-ray time units are malformed and all sampled ray times are zero.** The file's title also contains an unrelated 1970 date. A converter must prioritize verified coverage attributes and filenames, record scan-duration uncertainty, and avoid manufacturing individual ray times.
- Sample Nyquist velocity is about 16.81 m/s. Attributes do **not** establish the velocity dealiasing history. Do not label the archive velocity as reliably dealiased without further validation.

Inspection downloaded approximately 1.8 MB total (ZIP directory, one complete compressed sweep, and one small header fragment), not the 7 GB archive. Range checks used bytes `7030301428–7030432499`, `7029974842–7030432499`, `55–1193913`, and `7431847–7448230`. The last fragment was decompressed only far enough to inspect its complete metadata arrays; no padded meteorological data were interpreted.

## DOW access recheck

The accurate answer to “there is no DOW dataset?” is: **DOW6 and DOW8 collected El Reno data, and those data were used in published research. We have not found a verified direct public download for this event.** This is not evidence that the data are private, lost, or unobtainable.

The [2014 BAMS paper by Wurman, Kosiba, Robinson, and Marshall](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-13-00221.1) analyzes the 31 May 2013 event, including DOW6 and Rapid-Scan DOW measurements. The [2016 Wakimoto et al. paper](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/32144) independently documents the DOW8/RaXPol dataset used for its analyses. Its DOW8 description specifies six simultaneously transmitted, vertically stacked beams, 1° elevation separation beginning at 0.5°, approximately 0.9° beamwidth, 50 m range sampling, and approximately seven-second full-azimuth volume updates. These are event-paper specifications, not a verified inventory of downloadable files.

There is also a positive archive-access statement: the [2021 DOW population study](https://www.farmfacility.org/contents/pubs_pdf/2021-Wurman_etal-Supercell_tornadoes_are_stronger_and_wider_than_damage-based_ratings_indicate.pdf) describes FTP access through the publicly accessible DOW Facility archive, following facility instructions or contacting the authors. That general statement does not identify an El Reno file or guarantee its present unauthenticated availability.

The current [FARM access page](https://www.farmfacility.org/contents/facilitydata.php) provides the practical route: [**Request FARM Datasets**](https://forms.gle/CL8hExGHPHNq2oMq7). It lists FTP-backed major campaigns and directs inquiries for specialty tornado and hurricane datasets through that form. The page was retrieved directly over its working HTTP endpoint during the recheck; its HTTPS certificate did not validate in the command-line fetch. The request-form link was read from the retrieved page, then inspected without entering information or submitting anything. El Reno/ROTATE-2013 is not named in that page's campaign table.

A suitable eventual request would identify **ROTATE-2013, 31 May 2013 El Reno, DOW6 and DOW8/RSDOW**, and ask for the native reflectivity and radial-velocity sweeps, scan times/locations/elevations, calibration and dealiasing metadata, and terms for displaying derived map images. No request has been sent.

The bounded recheck covered official FARM/CSWR and Illinois archive references, the event papers, and indexed NCAR/EOL results. It did not uncover an event-specific downloadable raw asset. NCAR/EOL web access was limited by robots restrictions, and older facility links were not consistently reachable, so this is not an exhaustive proof that no public copy exists.

## Practical next step

AIR is the strongest presently verified downloadable supplement. A small 0.5° proof of concept should first verify time handling, projection, missing data, calibration, and velocity processing; any app layer must expose its own coverage and scan times. DOW and RaXPol are especially valuable scientifically but currently belong in the request/access-investigation category. No supplemental radar should replace the surveyed tornado path or be treated as an exact instantaneous tornado boundary.
