# Supplemental radar for 31 May 2013 El Reno

Checked 4 November 2026. This is an availability investigation, not an added app layer. No bulk radar archive was downloaded and no data request was sent.

## Best confirmed public download: AIR

The University of Oklahoma's [official curated archive](https://arrc.ou.edu/data.html) explicitly identifies its Atmospheric Imaging Radar (AIR) dataset as observations of the **31 May 2013 El Reno tornado and parent supercell**, from approximately 20–30 km range. This is AIR, not DOW or RaXPol. OU permits research, education, and outreach use with acknowledgment and appropriate radar/publication citations.

**Direct asset:** [20130531_AIR_CFRadial.zip](https://arrc.ou.edu/data/20130531_AIR_CFRadial.zip). HTTP HEAD returned 200, `application/zip`, 7,030,432,500 bytes, and byte-range support. The archive page describes CF/Radial format and links [Kurdzo et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-15-00266.1) for the radar observations.

Remote ZIP-directory inspection found **4,455 NetCDF sweep files, 99 volume timestamps**, from **23:11:56 through 23:24:51 UTC (6:11:56–6:24:51 PM CDT)**. Adjacent volume timestamps differ by 7 seconds (37 cases), 8 seconds (57), or 15 seconds (4). This public subset covers part of the tornado's lifetime; it begins after the current provisional closest-approach estimate from the camera track. Directory timestamps are volume starts, not independently verified ray times.

Limited sample inspection established:

- `cfrad.20130531_231156_AIR_v1_s6.nc` has a **0.5° fixed elevation**. The first sweep is −2°; choosing the first sweep would be incorrect for the requested 0.5° view.
- Sample variables include `DBZ` (reflectivity), `VEL` (radial velocity away from the instrument), and `WIDTH` (spectrum width).
- The first sampled sweep has 239 rays over roughly a 119° sector, approximately 0.5° azimuth sampling, and **30 m range-gate spacing**. Gate spacing is not a claim of independent spatial resolution or antenna beamwidth.
- Sample radar coordinates are approximately 35.31210, −97.74460; metadata altitude is 385 m.
- Global time coverage is readable: the first sampled sweep gives 23:11:56–23:12:04 UTC. **Per-ray time units are malformed and all sampled ray times are zero.** The file's title also contains an unrelated 1970 date. A converter must prioritize verified coverage attributes and filenames, record scan-duration uncertainty, and avoid manufacturing individual ray times.
- Sample Nyquist velocity is about 16.81 m/s. Attributes do **not** establish the velocity dealiasing history. Do not label the archive velocity as reliably dealiased without further validation.

Inspection downloaded approximately 1.8 MB total (ZIP directory, one complete compressed sweep, and one small header fragment), not the 7 GB archive. Range checks used bytes `7030301428–7030432499`, `7029974842–7030432499`, `55–1193913`, and `7431847–7448230`. The last fragment was decompressed only far enough to inspect its complete metadata arrays; no padded meteorological data were interpreted.

## Other instruments and access status

| Instrument | Event evidence | Verified availability and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DOW6 / DOW8** | A [primary AMS abstract](https://ams.confex.com/ams/27SLS/webprogram/Paper254162.html) identifies dual-polarization DOW6 and rapid-scan phased-array DOW8, alongside RaXPol, observing this tornado. The [2016 mobile-radar/damage paper](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/32144) provides scientific analyses. | The current [official FARM data page](https://www.farmfacility.org/contents/facilitydata.php) offers a request form and says specialty tornado datasets may be available. **No event-specific direct public raw download was verified.** Published plots are available, but are not interchangeable with native radial data. Actual event tilt schedules, coverage and processing would need the files or custodians' metadata; generic DOW specifications should not be substituted. |
| **RaXPol** | The [event-specific conference abstract](https://ams.confex.com/ams/27SLS/webprogram/Paper254379.html) reports three deployments, 360° scans every 2 seconds, 15 m range resolution, and elevations from 0° to 5°. The [multiscale study](https://doi.org/10.1175/WAF-D-14-00152.1) includes a 23:15:10 UTC, 3° example with dealiased velocity and truck-motion removal. | OU's [data policy](https://arrc.ou.edu/data.html) explicitly warns that pre-2017 RaXPol data availability is not guaranteed through RadarHub and gives a raw-data inquiry route. **No public event archive verified.** Paper figures are processed products; their dealiasing cannot be assumed for uninspected original data. |
| **NWRT phased-array radar (PAR; sometimes labeled MPAR in archives)** | [NSSL research](https://ams.confex.com/ams/94Annual/webprogram/Paper240997.html) describes approximately 60-second volumes within a 90° sector. An [NSSL event poster](https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/about/events/review2015/science/posters/Kuster_NSSLReview2015_poster.pdf) explicitly compares 0.5° reflectivity and velocity through tornadogenesis. | A [2025 paper's data statement](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-23-0110.1) links the [2013 NOAA THREDDS catalog](https://data.nssl.noaa.gov/thredds/catalog/RRDD/MPAR/2013/catalog.html), but a direct check returned **HTTP 410 Gone**. That is a documented historical raw-data location, not a verified functioning download. The [official NWS PAR GIF](https://www.weather.gov/images/oun/wxevents/20130531/radar/NWRT_20130531_ElReno.gif) remains linked from the event page: rendered imagery, not native radial data. |
| **KOUN / KCRI** | The [rapid-scan polarimetric study](https://doi.org/10.1175/WAF-D-15-0111.1) distinguishes these two research WSR-88Ds: KOUN used rapid sector scans; KCRI tested SAILS with 0.5° revisits about every 2–3 minutes. A [NOAA paper](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/26107/noaa_26107_DS1.pdf) documents El Reno KOUN samples at 0.52° at 23:03 and 0.97° at 23:11, with 250 m gate spacing and 0.5° azimuth increments. | Strong event evidence, **no event-specific public raw asset verified in this investigation**. These are additional WSR-88Ds, not mobile DOWs. Do not conflate KOUN with KCRI or assume that an image labeled “KOUN” proves an accessible archive. |
| **TOKC TDWR** | A [primary NSSL conference poster](https://ams.confex.com/ams/94Annual/webprogram/Handout/Paper233974/AMS2014_SevStmSym_Poster.pdf) identifies TOKC as useful high-resolution data for these May 2013 storms. The [2013 NWS distribution notice](https://www.weather.gov/media/notification/tins/tin13-03tdwr.pdf) names TOKC and says centrally collected TDWR-SPG products are archived at NCDC (now NCEI). | [NCEI radar inventory](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/nexradinv/index.jsp) is the archive discovery route. **May 31 files and product completeness were not verified.** TDWR-SPG Level III products are not equivalent to native TDWR Level II data; the [NWS technical notice](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/g/files/anmtlf171/files/2024-03/TDWR-Level-III-Products-Technical-Informatin-Notice.pdf) explicitly says TDWR Level II is not collected by that service. |
| **NOXP** | The same [primary NSSL poster](https://ams.confex.com/ams/94Annual/webprogram/Handout/Paper233974/AMS2014_SevStmSym_Poster.pdf) shows May 31 NOXP data near 23:22 UTC and identifies it as a mobile X-band dual-polarization radar. | Event observations confirmed; **no direct public event archive verified**. It is another potential custodian inquiry, not a currently ready layer. |
| **PX-1000** | OU's prominent public 2013 example is the **20 May Moore** tornado. | The [official archive](https://arrc.ou.edu/data.html) does not establish a May 31 El Reno download. Do not use Moore data as an El Reno supplement. |
| **CASA** | No May 31 El Reno acquisition was verified. A [primary 2014 CASA/NWP paper](https://ams.confex.com/ams/27SLS/webprogram/Manuscript/Paper255730/SLS2014_Brewster.pdf) describes moving the forecast domain from Oklahoma to Dallas/Fort Worth in spring 2012 in anticipation of radar relocation. | **No event-specific public dataset verified.** The former Oklahoma network's existence alone does not establish that it sampled El Reno in 2013. |

## Practical next step

AIR is the strongest presently verified downloadable supplement. A small 0.5° proof of concept should first verify time handling, projection, missing data, calibration, and velocity processing; any app layer must expose its own coverage and scan times. DOW and RaXPol are especially valuable scientifically but currently belong in the request/access-investigation category. No supplemental radar should replace the surveyed tornado path or be treated as an exact instantaneous tornado boundary.
